How this model works

1. Composite rating. Each of the 17 inputs is converted to a z-score against the league baselines above ((value − mean) / SD) and combined with your weights into a single Team Strength Index (TSI). ERA/WHIP/runs-allowed z-scores are inverted since lower is better there. Default weights aren't arbitrary — 16 of the 17 are each stat's actual correlation with winning, measured by backtesting the 2025 season game-by-game (2,117 games) in a separate pipeline: starting pitcher season ERA came out strongest (r = 0.148), then SP season WHIP (r = 0.106), then SP WHIP vs. the opposing lineup's active-roster batting AVG (r = 0.103), run differential and runs allowed per game, SP BB/9 and HR/9 vs. the opposing lineup's active-roster walk/HR rate, last-7-games team OPS, L10 win%, team OBP, bullpen ERA, season team OPS, runs scored per game, team AVG, team SLG, and a plain home-field flag (r = 0.065 at the bottom — see below). SP ERA (last 4 starts) is kept at a deliberately low, non-correlation-derived weight — its own measured correlation came out essentially at noise level (r ≈ 0.02–0.03), so the low weight was the right instinct all along. The three “vs. opposing lineup” stats are genuinely cross-team: team A's TSI reads team A's own SP rate stat AND team B's own batting rate stat, mixing different units (WHIP/HR9/BB9 are per-9-innings, AVG/HR-rate/BB-rate are per-at-bat) on purpose — there's no textbook formula being approximated, it's an empirically-found matchup signal, calibrated (mean/SD) directly off the real distribution of that diff in the 2025 backtest. Every stat except runs scored/allowed/differential, home field, and the starter's own numbers is built by summing only players on that team's current active roster — anyone hurt, optioned, DFA'd, or traded away is excluded, the same principle bullpen ERA already used, now applied to team batting too. Bullpen ERA sums earned runs and innings pitched across every reliever (zero starts this season) on today's active roster; team batting (AVG/OBP/SLG/OPS/HR-rate/BB-rate, season and last-7) sums the same way across today's active position players. Home field uses a plain “is this team home today” flag (r = 0.065) rather than each team's own home/away win% split (r = 0.046 in the same backtest) — a team's own home/away record so far this season is itself a small, noisy sample, while the universal “home teams win a bit more often” pattern held up as the more stable signal. Home win%/away win% below still get fetched and stay visible as reference info, they just no longer drive the composite.

2. Implied win%. Each team's TSI is passed through a logistic curve, dampened by the “confidence dampening” constant, so a strong composite edge still tops out around 65–70% — consistent with how much single-game variance real baseball has.

3. Head-to-head via log5. The two teams' implied win%s (pA, pB) are combined with Bill James' log5 formula: P(A) = (pA − pApB) / (pA + pB − 2pApB). This is the model's final win probability.

4. De-vig the market. If you enter both Kalshi prices, they're normalized to sum to 100% (priceA / (priceA + priceB)) so the comparison isn't partly just the exchange's spread.

5. Edge, EV, Kelly, fees, and contracts. Edge (the buy/pass decision) = model probability − de-vigged market probability, no fee involved — that's purely "is there a real mispricing." Once there is, everything about how much to risk switches to using price + Kalshi's real trading fee (0.07·price·(1−price), rounded up to the cent, charged at entry regardless of outcome) in place of price alone, since paying a fee is economically identical to the price itself being higher. EV per contract = model probability − (price + fee). Kelly fraction (f* = p − (1−p)·(price+fee)/(1−price−fee)) × your Kelly-fraction safety margin × your bankroll gives a dollar figure, which then gets divided by (price + fee) and rounded down to a whole number — Kalshi only sells whole contracts, so this never suggests spending more than you can actually place an order for.

7-day and 4-start samples are small and noisy — treat this as a decision-support heuristic to tune over time against your own results, not a guaranteed edge. Kalshi prices move; re-check before trading. Never stake more than you can afford to lose, and consider using a smaller Kelly fraction (e.g. 0.1–0.25) to dampen variance from a model this sensitive to small samples. “Fetch live data” pulls from MLB's public Stats API (statsapi.mlb.com), which is free and unauthenticated but not an official partner feed — spot-check anything that looks off before trading on it.
